The first release of DiskMap: see what's using your Mac's disk, with every file drawn as a rectangle sized by the space it takes.

## Download

Download **DiskMap-0.1.0-macOS-universal.zip** below, unzip it, and drag **DiskMap.app** into your Applications folder. It runs on Apple Silicon and Intel Macs with macOS 12 or later.

DiskMap isn't notarized by Apple yet, so macOS blocks it the first time you open it:

1. Open DiskMap. macOS says it can't verify the app. Click **Done**.
2. Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll down to the message about DiskMap, and click **Open Anyway**.
3. Confirm with your password or Touch ID. After that, DiskMap opens normally.

For a complete scan, also turn on **Full Disk Access** for DiskMap. The app explains how when you first open it.

## What's in it

- A fast, parallel scan of any folder or your whole disk
- A treemap of every file, coloured by type, with zoom
- A folder list sorted by size, and a file-type list you can click to highlight or isolate one type
- Built-in explanations of common space hogs, such as Xcode DerivedData, `node_modules`, caches and iPhone backups, plus Ask Claude and Google lookups for anything else
- Move to Trash (Put Back still works) or delete permanently, with confirmation and guards on system and home folders
- Light, dark, tinted and clear app icons that follow your Icon & widget style

## Known limitations

- The Intel version is built and included but hasn't been tested on an Intel Mac yet.
- Because the app isn't signed with an Apple Developer certificate, macOS may ask you to turn Full Disk Access on again after you install a new version.
